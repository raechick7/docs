A **Strategy** is the highest-level unit of work in Elliott Strategies. It represents a focused organizational goal — a clear statement of where you want to go and by when. Strategies give your team a shared point of reference, ensuring that every Initiative, Milestone, and Metric you create ultimately ties back to something that matters to the business.

## What a Strategy Contains

Each Strategy is made up of a structured set of fields that define its scope, ownership, and current state:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| **Title** | A short, memorable name for the strategic goal (e.g., "Grow Enterprise Revenue 30% YoY"). |
| **Description** | A longer explanation of the goal, its rationale, and what success looks like in plain language. |
| **Time Horizon** | The start and end date of the strategy — commonly a quarter, half-year, or full fiscal year. |
| **Owner** | The person or team accountable for driving the strategy forward and keeping it on track. |
| **Status** | The current state of the strategy (see the Status reference table below). |
| **Linked Initiatives** | The collection of Initiatives that represent the concrete work being done to achieve this strategy. |

## How Strategies Map to Organizational Goals

Strategies in Elliott Strategies are intentionally broad by design. They are not project plans — they are the *reason* your projects exist. A well-formed Strategy answers three questions:

1. **What** are we trying to achieve?
2. **Why** does it matter to the organization right now?
3. **How will we know** when we have succeeded?

You answer the first two questions in the title and description. You answer the third by attaching Metrics and linking Initiatives that have clear, measurable outcomes. This keeps every level of the hierarchy anchored to organizational intent, not just activity.

<Note>
Elliott Strategies recommends maintaining **one to three active Strategies per team or business unit** at any given time. Running too many Strategies simultaneously dilutes focus and makes it harder to track meaningful progress. Most teams align their Strategy cadence to annual planning cycles, with quarterly check-ins to reassess status and priorities.
</Note>

## Strategy Status Values

Use the **Status** field to communicate where a Strategy stands at a glance. Update it regularly as circumstances change.

| Status | Description |
|---|---|
| **Draft** | The Strategy is being defined and has not yet been shared with the broader team. It is not actively being executed. |
| **Active** | The Strategy is approved, in progress, and being actively worked on by one or more linked Initiatives. |
| **On Hold** | Execution has been paused — typically due to a change in priorities, resourcing constraints, or external blockers. |
| **Completed** | The Strategy has reached its end date and its goals have been met or formally closed out. |
| **Archived** | The Strategy is no longer relevant and has been removed from active views. Historical data is preserved. |

## Best Practices for Naming Strategies

<Tip>
Write Strategy titles as **outcome statements**, not activity descriptions. Instead of "Marketing Campaign for Q3," write "Increase Brand Awareness Among Mid-Market Buyers in Q3." Outcome-oriented names remind your team *why* the work matters, make progress easier to assess, and help stakeholders quickly understand what each Strategy is trying to accomplish.
</Tip>
