Elliott Strategies organizes everything you do around a single, coherent data model. Instead of scattering goals across spreadsheets, slide decks, and project tools, the platform gives each piece of work a defined place in a hierarchy — so you always know how a day-to-day task connects to a company-wide objective, and whether that objective is actually being achieved.

## The Core Hierarchy

Every account in Elliott Strategies lives inside a **Workspace** — your organization's shared environment. Inside that Workspace, your work flows from the broadest level of intent down to the smallest measurable signal:

- **Workspace** — Your organization's shared environment. All members, data, and settings live here.
- **Strategies** — High-level goals that define where your organization is heading over a given time horizon. A Workspace can contain multiple Strategies running in parallel (e.g., one per business unit or planning cycle).
- **Initiatives** — Concrete projects or workstreams that live inside a Strategy. Each Initiative is the actionable "how" behind the strategic "what."
- **Milestones & Tasks** — Structured checkpoints and individual work items that break an Initiative into manageable, trackable steps.
- **Metrics** — Quantitative measurements attached to a Strategy or Initiative that tell you whether you are making real progress. Metrics can track leading or lagging indicators and update manually or via integrations.

This top-down structure means every Milestone and every Metric always traces back to a Strategy — giving leadership a live picture of execution health without requiring manual status reporting.

## How the Pieces Connect

A **Strategy** is the container for a focused organizational goal — for example, "Expand into the European market by Q4." Inside that Strategy, you create one or more **Initiatives**, each representing a distinct project that contributes to the goal, such as "Launch localized product pages" or "Hire regional sales team."

Each Initiative can be broken into **Milestones** (major checkpoints, like "Complete translations by March 1") and **Tasks** (individual work items assigned to team members). At both the Strategy and Initiative level, you attach **Metrics** — numerical targets like "Website conversion rate ≥ 3%" or "Qualified leads generated ≥ 500" — so you always have an objective answer to the question, *"Is this working?"*

<Info>
You do not need to populate every level of the hierarchy before you start. Many teams begin with a Strategy and a few Initiatives, then add Milestones and Metrics as execution gets underway.
</Info>
