A **Metric** is a quantitative measurement you attach to a Strategy or Initiative to answer one critical question: *Is this actually working?* Metrics transform your strategic plans from a collection of intentions into a live performance picture — one where you can see, at any point in time, whether your team is on track to hit its targets or whether something needs to change. Without Metrics, a Strategy is just a statement of hope.

## Types of Metrics: Leading vs. Lagging Indicators

Not all measurements are created equal. Elliott Strategies supports two distinct types of metrics, and using both gives you the clearest possible view of your execution health.

**Lagging indicators (KPIs)** measure outcomes after the fact. They tell you whether you achieved a goal, but they do not tell you *why* or give you early warning when things go wrong.

**Leading indicators** measure inputs and early signals that predict future outcomes. They give you the ability to course-correct while there is still time to act.

<Accordion title="Examples of Lagging Indicators (KPIs)">
- Annual recurring revenue (ARR) growth
- Net Revenue Retention (NRR) at end of quarter
- Customer churn rate (monthly)
- Employee retention rate (annual)
- Number of enterprise deals closed
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Examples of Leading Indicators">
- Number of qualified sales opportunities created this week
- Average response time to inbound leads
- Weekly active users in the product
- Number of customer check-in calls completed
- Onboarding completion rate for new hires
</Accordion>

Build your measurement framework with a mix of both types. Leading indicators let you act early; lagging indicators confirm whether your actions produced the right results.

## Linking a Metric to a Strategy or Initiative

You can attach a Metric at two levels in the hierarchy:

- **Strategy-level Metrics** measure the overall outcome of the Strategy. These are typically lagging indicators — the big-picture numbers that tell you if the Strategy succeeded.
- **Initiative-level Metrics** measure the impact of a specific project. These are often leading indicators that contribute to the Strategy-level KPIs.

To link a Metric, open the Strategy or Initiative you want to track, navigate to the **Metrics** tab, and select **Add Metric**. You can create a new Metric from scratch or attach an existing one that is already defined in your Workspace.

## Metric Fields

Each Metric is defined by a set of fields that make it precise, comparable, and easy to update:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| **Name** | A short, descriptive label for the metric (e.g., "Monthly Qualified Leads"). |
| **Description** | An explanation of what the metric measures, how it is calculated, and why it matters. |
| **Unit** | The unit of measurement — common options include `%`, `$`, `count`, `days`, `score`, or a custom label. |
| **Target Value** | The number you are aiming to reach by the end of the measurement period (e.g., `500` qualified leads). |
| **Current Value** | The most recent recorded value for this metric. Updated manually or via an integration. |
| **Frequency** | How often the metric is measured and updated — e.g., daily, weekly, monthly, or quarterly. |

## Manual Updates vs. Automated Integrations

<Note>
Metrics in Elliott Strategies can be updated in two ways. **Manual updates** mean a team member enters the current value directly on a set cadence — useful for metrics that come from external reports or require human judgment. **Automated updates** pull data directly from connected tools (such as your CRM, analytics platform, or data warehouse) so your Metrics stay current without any manual effort. Check the Integrations section to see which data sources are available for your plan.
</Note>

## Keep Your Metrics Focused

<Tip>
Resist the urge to track everything. Teams that attach ten or more Metrics to a single Strategy often find that the data becomes noise — no one knows which numbers to act on, and updates fall behind. Aim for **three to five Metrics per Strategy**, each chosen because it directly signals whether the strategic goal is being achieved. If a metric does not change the decisions you make, it does not belong on your Strategy.
</Tip>
